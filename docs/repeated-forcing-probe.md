# Repeated prescribed-water forcing probe: milestone C3o

Status: implemented as an observational headless developer probe, `cyclic-prescribed-forcing-probe-1`, September 29, 2026. The water solver and its `seeded-multi-entry-network-4` checkpoint semantics are **unchanged**. This milestone tests the limits of the current model; it does not make repeated forcing generally reliable or add a desktop water cycle.

## Contract

`seeded_forcing_probe` generates one versioned world and repeatedly applies a finite list of prescribed regional input profiles in cyclic order. Each profile supplies constant inputs during one **normalized interval**. The sequence is explicit and deterministic; these volumes are not climate-derived precipitation, calibrated daily runoff or physically timed discharge. The [reference request](scenarios/seeded-forcing-probe.json) has two alternating distributed profiles, 100 intervals and a checkpoint after interval 50.

The compact report records the resolved recipe, forcing profiles, solver and probe versions, completed intervals, first rejected interval and reason, authoritative initial/accepted/final water volumes, maximum absolute full-ledger residual, event count and optional Linux process peak RSS. It also distinguishes the generator's fitted initial volume from the dynamic model's sum of exclusive initial stocks; a few cubic meters of floating-point reduction difference are retained as a diagnostic, not silently corrected. A [second fixture](scenarios/seeded-forcing-small-input.json) uses the same regions and cycle with every input divided by 20,000.

At `replayAfter`, the probe serializes the **complete** Rust checkpoint and restores it, then compares every later interval result and checkpoint exactly against uninterrupted execution. An interval rejection must leave both models at their previous checkpoint; otherwise the probe fails as an internal error. Rejections are reported as data, not omitted seeds. Replay comparison happens only after the requested checkpoint; an earlier rejection cannot claim replay coverage. The report does not embed the huge checkpoint or every interval event.

Requests are limited to 32 KiB, 1–1,000 intervals, 1–16 nonempty profiles, and at most 64 regional inputs per profile. Existing generated-world and solver bounds still apply. A full large checkpoint is serialized **inside the probe** for verification; the separate `seeded_network` CLI still cannot accept that multi-megabyte checkpoint through its 32 KiB request limit. This is not a persistent desktop save API.

```sh
cargo run --release --locked --manifest-path native/Cargo.toml --example seeded_forcing_probe -- docs/scenarios/seeded-forcing-probe.json
```

## Fixed-seed evidence and exact failure witness

For `first-light` at subdivision 5 (10,242 regions), 100 alternating intervals completed, with 4 × 10^14 m³ of accepted external input, final exclusive storage 7,906,994,258,411,542 m³, full-ledger residual 7 m³ and maximum absolute residual 8 m³. A 2,593,634-byte checkpoint after interval 50 restored exactly, and all 50 subsequent interval reports and checkpoints matched. One release-process run reported about 409 ms elapsed and 29,324 KiB peak RSS; these include generation, validation, replay and serialization, not isolated solver throughput.

The [complete fixed 20-seed run](data/seeded-forcing-probe.csv), with only the seed changed, accepted all 100 intervals for **12 seeds**. Eight seeds stopped at their first rejected interval (numbers 2, 4, 8, 20, 43, 83 or 84), all with `Concurrent event budget does not balance.` No failed seed was filtered or retried. Every reported rejection left its last accepted checkpoint unchanged. The maximum absolute full-ledger residual over all completed states in this sample was 22 m³, within the existing relative tolerance. This is an engineering stress sample, not a measured probability of failure in arbitrary worlds.

One retained witness is `profile-15`, interval 2. At its second internal event, supplied segment volume was approximately 8,175,431,636.646 m³, while the sum of **represented stock differences** was 8,175,431,636.6615 m³. The 0.0155029296875 m³ discrepancy exceeded that event's existing 0.008175431637 m³ tolerance. Three active stocks received approximately 2.044 × 10^9, 2.044 × 10^9 and 4.088 × 10^9 m³ of represented increments. The evidence points to floating-point rounding when differences are taken between larger stocks; it does not justify dropping the discrepancy or raising a tolerance without a separate accounting design. The entire interval was rejected atomically. A separate `10^-4` m³ input into a large initialized world is also rejected as below stock precision with zero accepted external input. These are different numerical problems; a source-side pending queue would not automatically resolve the internal event residual.

The [smaller-input 20-seed run](data/seeded-forcing-small-input.csv) completed all 100 intervals for 13 seeds; seven rejected a `Shared level cannot represent the requested storage precisely.` check, six at interval 1 and one at interval 2. `profile-03` provides an exact diagnostic: one branch requested 50,000,000 m³ but reconstruction from the represented absolute water elevation returned about 50,000,000.00963 m³, above that stock's 0.005 m³ reconstruction tolerance. Its level was near -4,053.65 m and wetted area near 4.66 × 10^10 m²; one `f64` step in absolute elevation corresponds to an order of 0.02 m³ of volume there. This is a **level-representation** problem, distinct from the internal event-budget failure and from the `10^-4` m³ stock-precision example. The 50-million-m³ input is an order-of-magnitude probe for regional water flux, not modeled rainfall.

Validation on September 29, 2026: `npm test`, Rust formatting, Clippy across all targets with warnings denied, and `npm run test:desktop` passed. The desktop fingerprint remained `2e66ac09`. Tests cover bounded and malformed requests, exact replay after serialized checkpoint, atomic sub-precision rejection, and both retained generated-world failure witnesses. No renderer or native wire-protocol behavior changed; the existing Vite chunk-size advisory remains.

## Next decision

Do not enable continuous water forcing in the desktop yet. A candidate correction must explicitly account for represented-stock roundoff and any remainder across events and checkpoints, preserve the initial/external ledgers, define its acceptance bound independently of the global stock size, and be separately versioned. The water-level inverse additionally needs a numerically justified representation check that accounts for achievable elevation precision without changing the stored water volume. Compare changes against both retained 20-seed sweeps, smaller directed reservoirs and serialized continuation. Small-input pending storage requires its own ownership and temporal-release policy; [C3h's pending-water requirements](spill-readiness.md) still apply. No climate, erosion, river discharge or drying/splitting is implemented here.
