# Versioned water accounting: design gate

Status: design proposal with isolated arithmetic and exclusive-merge experiments plus a separately versioned [read-only exact initial-water import](exact-initial-accounting.md), not an implemented exact dynamic solver or production checkpoint format. Updated September 30, 2026. This record separates the product requirement of conserved, reproducible water from candidate arithmetic mechanisms. No desktop dynamics are enabled by it.

## Why the current representation is insufficient

The current seeded solver stores one `f64` volume per exclusive active branch. It also uses `f64` for interval input, capacity and event coordinates. This is adequate for the bounded experiments that pass their precision checks, but adding a small positive grant to a large stock can round the stock back to its old value. Conversely, a represented stock difference can exceed its nominal grant. [The measured C3q event](event-budget-roundoff.md) differs by 0.0155029296875 m³ while its existing event tolerance is about 0.008175432 m³. Moving a nonlimiting stock by one representable step cannot repair that event. The current atomic rejection is preferable to silently changing the input or widening the tolerance.

The needed property is not “more decimal places everywhere.” It is an explicit, spatially owned account of every accepted input and represented stock change, including sub-ULP contributions and rounded allocations. A derived water level may stay visually unchanged until enough volume accumulates; the underlying volume must still be retained.

## Required invariants for a new version

1. **Local ownership.** Any unrepresented contribution belongs to an active basin stock or to a specified, checkpointed source queue. A global unlocated remainder cannot be routed correctly after a frontier changes.
2. **Conservation.** For every accepted event and interval, the model can reconcile external input with exclusive active storage, including explicit pending water if that policy exists. An approximate display total is not the accounting authority.
3. **Capacity and topology.** A branch may saturate only at its validated capacity. Merge operations transfer the entire compensated content of children to the parent without duplication, disappearance or premature access to a new frontier.
4. **Atomicity.** Invalid, overflowing or unresolved updates leave the complete checkpoint unchanged. Zero and very small positive inputs have explicit behavior; no implicit discard or unbounded retry is allowed.
5. **Replay.** Checkpoints include every low-order component, pending amount, counter and policy version needed for exact same-build continuation. V1–v5 checkpoints and their prior semantics remain readable under their own versions; no silent migration pretends to recover precision already lost.
6. **Bounded error.** Rounding bounds must be attached to local operations and reported diagnostics, not scaled only by the planet's total water. Conservation tests include long runs and adversarial size ratios, not just one successful world.
7. **Separation.** Arithmetic changes physical state only through an explicit model transition. Rendering and visual level precision never feed back into stock accounting.

## Candidate mechanisms to compare

| Candidate | Advantage | Main risk / proof obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compensated high/low volume per active branch | Retains sub-ULP grants near very large stocks; locality follows the branch | Every threshold, merge, total and checkpoint operation must use the pair correctly; rounding in rate/grant allocation still needs a policy |
| Exact fixed-point volume with bounded unit | Straightforward integer accounting and replay | The unit must cover tiny inputs and huge worlds without overflow; geometric capacities and ratios remain floating-point |
| Local rational multiples of a fixed unit | Exact equal splits can stay symmetric while each branch owns its fraction | Denominators and intermediate numerators can grow or overflow; ties, rate changes, merge rules and checkpoint bounds need a policy |
| Explicit pending volume per source or receiving branch | Makes delayed water visible and locatable | Release timing can change which route is open; source identity and merge behavior become part of the physical model |

These are proposals, not interchangeable implementations. In particular, compensated stock addition alone does **not** guarantee that separately rounded grants sum to the event input. The event-coordinate, rate split, limiting endpoint and final remainder must be reconciled under one documented rule. A deterministic last-recipient remainder is one candidate only if it stays nonnegative, respects capacity, and has a proven local deviation bound; otherwise the event must remain atomic or carry an explicitly owned pending amount.

## Evaluation order

1. Build a small, isolated arithmetic experiment with independent reference values: a large stock plus many sub-ULP inputs, one exact threshold, a two-child merge, and the retained three-recipient `profile-15` event. Measure representation error and performance. Do not wire it into the desktop or silently replace the existing `Stock` type.
2. Choose the representation and event-remainder policy from these outcomes. Define a distinct checkpoint schema/version, including strict validation and serialization round-trip tests, before modifying the generated-world path.
3. Integrate behind an explicit opt-in version. Compare directed reservoirs, the 20-seed large/small repeated-forcing sweeps, unchanged legacy replay, atomic rejection and exact serialized continuation. Keep failures in the result set rather than selecting only successful seeds.
4. Run longer, varied-resolution tests and measure runtime/memory. Only then decide whether the new version is sound enough to become the generated-world default and support a desktop demonstration.

Passing one 20-seed sweep is a regression milestone, **not** a scientific or universal reliability claim. Climate-derived forcing, finite-time discharge, drying/splitting and ocean dynamics have separate requirements.

The [test-only arithmetic experiment](../native/tests/compensated_water_experiment.rs) now covers several narrow cases. A single `f64` stock at 5 × 10^14 m³ loses each of 1,024 grants of 1/1,024 m³; an experimental high/low pair reaches the exact +1 m³ outcome even across JSON serialization. A sub-ULP remainder is visible in a capacity comparison. The three measured `profile-15` grants reconstruct their nominal event input when stock-addition roundoff is retained locally. Ten thousand deterministic dyadic signed grants, with repeated JSON round-trips, agree at every step with an independent `i128` ledger in units of 1/1,024 m³. This is an arithmetic test, not a model of negative rainfall.

The two-child merge test exposes a broader representation requirement: two individually representable child capacities have an exact sum that differs from their rounded single-`f64` parent birth volume. A new exclusive-parent state must preserve that difference, so merely replacing the active `Stock` field while leaving branch birth and merge arithmetic as single floats is insufficient. A separate three-way rate split shows that ordinary `f64` summation can equal the event input while compensated summation exposes a nonzero residual. A canonical last-recipient remainder balances this controlled case, but an already saturated last recipient could not accept it; the general recipient/ownership rule is still open.

An additional closed three-child laboratory transition now validates that supplied grants sum exactly in the compensated representation, applies them atomically against capacities, and merges only when every child is exactly full. In a controlled one-third split, the canonical third share leaves its branch one binary unit below capacity; a later explicit input fills it and only then activates the parent. A halfway JSON round-trip produces the same parent state, and invalid nonconservative or over-capacity events leave the prior state unchanged. The final parent volume also matches an independent `i128` dyadic-unit sum of the three child capacities. This laboratory validates **given grants**; it does not derive grants from rates or route water through a real basin graph.

The next test-only candidate now constructs three grants from positive rates. Limiting grants remain exactly at their specified capacity deficits. The last nonlimiting recipient owns the exact high/low remainder; a bounded search may move the other nonlimiting nominal grants by up to eight neighboring `f64` values to find a positive, capacity-safe split. Every returned plan is checked to conserve the supplied high/low input exactly and to stay within eight nominal ULPs per recipient. This bound is an **experimental rejection guard**, not a validated physical error model or a production policy. If no nonlimiting owner exists and fixed grants do not sum to the input, the candidate rejects explicitly. The measured `profile-15` three-recipient event and controlled exact-endpoint cases pass; a far-from-nominal limiting grant rejects. A simple unadjusted last-recipient rule failed a controlled capacity case despite a nearby feasible split, motivating the bounded search.

An adversarial relabeling test exposes a stronger limitation. For three identical capacities and equal rates, the canonical remainder owner makes one physical child underfilled and another exactly full; merely permuting otherwise identical branch IDs changes **which** child is full. This can change later routing, not just a display number. Therefore this canonical-owner candidate is **not acceptable as a production policy as written**. A controlled alternative spreads the tiny capacity deficit across all three high/low stocks: exact input remains conserved and every child remains below the threshold, so the immediate saturation topology is symmetric. Its last low-order share still differs by a tiny amount. Exact bitwise equality of all three shares and exact conservation of a finite binary input divided by three cannot both hold with finite binary per-stock numbers; a future policy must specify the permitted low-order asymmetry or a shared, spatially owned remainder.

A stricter **test-only** allocation variant now requires every recipient not explicitly marked limiting to retain positive capacity headroom; marked limiting recipients must receive their exact deficit. The earlier search over neighboring `f64` grants alone rejects the equal-rate three-way case, so a narrow fallback distributes its positive aggregate headroom among the three compensated grants and checks the same conservation, capacity and local-deviation bounds. The equal-rate case then leaves all three children below capacity; an explicitly marked endpoint remains full; an exact joint endpoint with no marked limiting recipient rejects rather than silently merging. A raw search still accepted or rejected the same near-capacity input differently when recipients were reordered. The variant therefore sorts recipients by rate, remaining capacity and limiting status before assigning a remainder. All six permutations of one controlled limiting case with distinct numeric keys preserve both the physical saturation mask and exact per-recipient grants. A 256-case deterministic near-capacity sample contained both acceptances and rejections with no acceptance-status disagreement across its six permutations; the same sample retains at least one raw-order disagreement as a regression witness. These checks are not a general permutation proof. In the equal-rate case the actual low-order grants still change when labels are permuted, so later trajectories may diverge; event-time selection and a fully relabel-stable state policy remain unresolved. Neither allocation variant is integrated into the solver.

A two-event continuation makes this limit operational. After the accepted equal-rate event, the three identical children have slightly different positive deficits. Supplying each with the same compensated amount equal to the smallest deficit is a representable, exactly summed external input, but one updated child would require a residual below the two-float precision available at its stock magnitude. The checked laboratory transition rejects the entire second event and preserves its checkpoint. Canonical sorting therefore does not make this tie continuously traversable; it prevents a silent topology error at the cost of an explicit unsupported step. A representation or spatially owned pending policy that can handle this case is still needed before a production version.

A separate [test-only fixed-point experiment](../native/tests/fixed_point_water_experiment.rs) checks one alternative without changing the solver. With `i128` units of 2^-56 m³, exact conversion of the current volume-recipe ceiling at the maximum supported radius leaves more than 16-fold raw integer headroom. This does **not** cover every future stock, cumulative input, coverage-derived water volume, geometric capacity, or sub-unit input; conversion below the declared unit rejects explicitly. At the three-equal-child fixture, the exact aggregate capacity exceeds the 100 m³ input by 512 integer units. Splitting this headroom into positive deficits conserves the input, and a further equal grant crosses the previously unrepresentable two-float step without losing a unit. But 512 is not divisible by three: exact equal per-child stocks are impossible in this fixed unit, and the remainder assignment determines which physical child fills first. Thus fixed point removes this precision rejection but **does not, by itself, solve relabel-stable tied routing**. The remaining design decision is whether a validated microscopic asymmetry is acceptable, or whether tied recipients need an explicit symmetric group/owned remainder policy with defined behavior when the tie breaks.

Initial-state import is a separate exactness gate. In a fixed 20-seed subdivision-2 sample using 71% coverage, every current floating-point branch stock and global initial volume converts to the experimental 2^-56 m³ unit and fits the `i128` sum, but **4 of 20** exact branch sums differ from the separately rounded global total; the largest difference is 153.5625 m³. The existing importer validates its own approximate tolerance, not exact equality. A future version must specify which represented quantities are authoritative, record the reconciliation against the legacy total, and reject imports outside a declared bound. Simply casting the existing branch stocks and global total into an exact type cannot produce a conserved initial checkpoint. This sample is a regression witness, not an error distribution or a proposed reconciliation bound.

The opt-in [exact initial accounting report](exact-initial-accounting.md) now chooses the represented **regional** `area × depth` contributions as its local initial authority, sums them in `2^-56 m³` integer units, and records the difference from separately rounded legacy totals. It audits source sums against a conservative analytical floating-summation bound and rejects contributions outside its representable range. This closes only the read-only import question for supported inputs; it does not choose an event-remainder policy, move water, validate long-running forcing, or turn the report into a dynamic checkpoint.

One more controlled fixture shows that the symmetry conflict is about representation and ownership, not a mathematical impossibility of conserving a tied split. If each of the three local stocks stores a numerator in thirds of the fixed unit, the same integer external input adds an identical numerator to every child. After 510 of the 512 remaining units arrive, all three remain equally below capacity by 2/3 of a unit; the final two units fill all three simultaneously. Their summed local numerators exactly match the external ledger at both checkpoints. This is only a narrow group-denominator witness. Arbitrary tie sizes, changing rates, separating or merging groups, denominator growth, range loss from larger numerators, geometric capacities, checkpoint encoding, and future routing remain unmodeled. It is not a proposed production implementation yet.

A follow-up local-rational fixture permits a real symmetry break: one child receives an additional whole unit, then all three receive equal shares of 509 units. The addressed child reaches capacity while the other two remain one unit short; separately filling them retains the exact external ledger. This tests supplied grants and local ownership, not event-time selection or a route through basin geometry. The same rational arithmetic reconstructs each parent amount through 80 successive three-way splits of one unit, then explicitly rejects the 81st split because its `i128` denominator would overflow. Larger numerators and cross-products can hit limits sooner. A production scheme cannot assume arbitrary exact fractions will remain bounded through a long simulation; it must specify limits, normalization, refusal or an alternative owned-remainder mechanism before checkpoints or routing are designed around it.

A closed three-recipient event-time fixture now uses those local rational stocks with **positive integer rate weights**. It selects the minimum exact external-input coordinate `(capacity - stock) × total_rate / recipient_rate`, grants each recipient its rate share, and verifies the grants sum to that coordinate without exceeding any capacity. Three equal recipients jointly fill after the remaining 512 fixed-point units. If one physically receives one extra unit first, only that recipient fills at the 509-unit coordinate; all six receiver permutations recover the same event coordinate and physical post-event stocks. A separate 1:2:3 rate fixture with capacities 10, 20 and 60 units detects a simultaneous first-and-second-recipient limit at 60 units rather than giving an ID priority, again across all six permutations. Rate-sum overflow rejects. These are directed arithmetic facts, **not** a general event solver: real computed rates are floating-point, groups and frontiers change, and there is no routing, time duration, storage curve or production checkpoint in this fixture.

A further **test-only closed tie-lifecycle** fixture explicitly changes its exclusive frontier. Three equal-rate children can fill simultaneously and become one parent, without allocating an indivisible fixed-point remainder to an arbitrary child. Alternatively, one physically addressed extra unit fills that child first; the next event excludes only that full child, then the other two fill and the parent activates. The same physical stock trajectory and final parent amount hold across all six child-ID permutations. An exact local input ledger balances both child and parent frontiers, a JSON round-trip resumes between the two events, and invalid rates, over-capacity input, premature child reactivation and corrupted ledger/frontier checkpoints reject without changing the committed state. The parent accepts a further bounded input after the merge. This closes one deliberately tiny **provided-rate, closed-group lifecycle** only. It does not select recipients, calculate geographic flow or levels, route overflow, handle arbitrary merge trees, or establish a bounded-denominator policy for a production checkpoint.

The event fixture also checks a narrow bridge from positive `f64` rates to exact integer weights: decompose each binary64 value into a significand and power of two, align exponents, then reduce the common integer factor. Thus rates `[1, nextUp(1), 2]` become exact weights `[2^52, 2^52 + 1, 2^53]`; the slightly faster receiver reaches an otherwise identical capacity first for a physical numerical reason. The conversion explicitly rejects zero/nonfinite rates and an extreme `10^-300 : 1 : 10^300` range that cannot fit `i128`. Preserving the exact **represented** `f64` ratios does not prove the upstream rate calculation physically accurate. Intermediate rational products and event coordinates may also overflow for accepted weights; a versioned model would need bounded inputs and atomic rejection or a different representation.

Common factors must be removed **before** aligning significands into `i128`. The rates `[3, 3 × 2^126, 3]` have exact reduced weights `[1, 2^126, 1]`, including a valid sum, although the unreduced middle weight exceeds `i128`. This is a representational acceptance boundary, not an extension of the model's physical range.

The event fixture also exposed an avoidable intermediate overflow. With equal capacities of `2^80` units and weights `[2^60, 1, 1]`, the first event is at `2^80 + 2^21` input units and grants `[2^80, 2^20, 2^20]` units. Computing every candidate as `deficit × total_rate / rate` first overflows `i128`, including for a nonlimiting receiver whose event is much later. The laboratory now compares `deficit / rate` before multiplying only the earliest coordinate by `total_rate`, and cancels rate factors before computing grants. All six receiver permutations give the same physical result. This prevents that **avoidable** failure, but rational denominators, final coordinates and cumulative ledgers still have finite bounds. It does not justify accepting arbitrary input scales or replacing the generated-world solver.

The same fixture now compares nonnegative rational thresholds by Euclidean quotients rather than overflowing cross-products. A small exhaustive integer-domain reference and a near-`i128::MAX` directed pair verify the ordering. This removes a comparison-only bound; it does not remove bounds on representing the event or its individual stocks.

In a deterministic 512-case smoke sample with positive varied rates, no limiting recipients and generous individual capacities, the guarded candidate accepted 505 cases and rejected seven. Every accepted plan had positive capacity-safe grants and an exactly matching high/low event total. This is only a code-path sample; it is not a reliability estimate, and the label-bias witness disqualifies direct integration regardless of its acceptance count.

The two-float representation also has a finite precision boundary. With a 5 × 10^14 m³ high component and a 10^-300 m³ low component, adding another positive 10^-320 m³ changes neither component in the unchecked experiment. A laboratory checked-add candidate now detects this loss and rejects the whole exclusive event without changing its checkpoint, including the event counter and input ledger. At the same time, the pair exposes a 1/1,024 m³ capacity deficit that a single `f64` high component reports as zero. These directed tests establish a necessary behavior at one adversarial scale; they do not establish general exactness, a suitable minimum representable input, or a production policy for smaller contributions. A future solver must define whether such inputs are rejected or retained in an explicitly owned queue, and validate the choice across its supported volume range.

Checkpoint decoding needs semantic validation beyond successful JSON parsing. A deliberately malformed pair `{high: 99, low: 2}` passes ordinary deserialization, but a lexicographic high/low comparison incorrectly reports it below a capacity of 100 even though the represented sum is 101. Negative zero creates another comparison ambiguity. The laboratory now checks canonical pair normalization, finite positive capacities, exclusive child/parent state, per-child capacity, and equality with its input ledger before resuming an event; the malformed checkpoint rejects atomically. This is a test-only validation sketch, not a production checkpoint schema. A future schema also needs version, full topology and geometry consistency, bounded counters, and independent corrupted-data tests.

These observations do **not** select a production representation. High/low storage has a tied-threshold precision refusal; fixed integer units have an indivisible-remainder identity bias; unbounded local rational units eventually exceed finite integer range. None proves general rate-to-grant reconciliation, arbitrary merge-tree correctness, safe normalization at extreme scales, or checkpoint validity. The next gate is an explicit ownership and tie-lifecycle policy together with precise event-time accounting, followed by varied-ensemble and cost measurements. Do not move any current candidate into the generated-world solver.
