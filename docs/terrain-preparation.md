# Bounded dry terrain preparation

Status: implemented in generated `terrain-prep-1` worlds and opt-in `continuous-plates-1` worlds, and exposed by the desktop. The native kernel is also independently tested. Existing `basins-1` recipes retain their original bed, water, drainage, basin analysis, protocol-9 layout, and fingerprint. Preparation does not represent elapsed geological time or climate-driven erosion.

The operation provides a material-accounted bed change before initial water filling. The kernel accepts a closed spherical region graph, physical edge distances, reference-sphere region areas, initial bed elevations, and at most 16 preparation passes. It returns a prepared elevation field, gross eroded and deposited thickness per region, gross transported reference-area-equivalent volume, and requested/applied pass counts. It does not mutate its input.

For one undirected edge with high region `a`, low region `b`, distance `d`, areas `A_a` and `A_b`, and snapshot heights `h_a > h_b`, the candidate transport volume is:

```text
excess = max(0, h_a - h_b - 0.004 × d)
candidate_volume = 0.25 × excess / (1/A_a + 1/A_b)
```

The dimensionless `0.004` threshold is a **mesh-scale smoothing heuristic**, not a measured talus angle. Initial tests at subdivisions 2–4 found generated maximum neighbor slopes of approximately 0.0026–0.0090 in three fixed worlds; a 0.01 threshold did nothing in all three. A later [12-seed sensitivity study](terrain-preparation-study.md) found strong resolution dependence and active per-pass caps. The coefficients remain uncalibrated.

Each pass evaluates all edges from the same beginning-of-pass heights. Candidate volumes are summed by donor and receiver. A transfer is then scaled by the smaller of its donor's and receiver's available factors, so gross removal and gross deposition each remain at most 100 m per region per pass. The identical scaled volume is subtracted from one region and added to the other after division by their areas. This preserves the reference-area material ledger within floating-point error. Material is deposited in the same pass; there is no untracked suspended stock or export. Pass order and edge traversal are deterministic for a fixed graph.

This is not a physical sediment model: bed elevation is used as a surrogate material column with uniform density, areas ignore topographic slope, and there is no regolith, grain size, moisture, vegetation, or tectonic replenishment. A pass has no year/day duration. The routine validates finite heights, positive areas/distances, bounded pass count, and reciprocal edges with matching distances. Invalid inputs reject without modifying the supplied bed.

`terrain-prep-1` and `continuous-plates-1` require `terrainPreparationPasses` in the range 0–16; the desktop form defaults to four passes under `terrain-prep-1`. Generation applies the dry operation after crust/plate/detail elevation and before initial water fitting, then computes drainage and basins from the prepared bed. The per-region signed difference is the fifth elevation contribution. Protocol 10 adds that Float64 array and records applied passes and gross transported volume in the world header. The resolved initial-world export uses format version 2. The world fingerprint includes the resolved recipe and prepared fields. `basins-1` does not accept the new parameter or silently migrate.

Directed tests cover an analytically known three-region transfer, no movement below threshold, simultaneous per-region caps, malformed graphs and non-finite fields, deterministic repeated runs, area/datum/relabeling invariance, and area-weighted material accounting on seeded generated worlds. Integration tests compare zero-pass worlds with legacy fields, reconstruct the prepared elevation, independently rebuild water/drainage/basins, and replay a water-only checkpoint on the prepared world. A [reproducible ensemble](terrain-preparation-study.md) measures 12 seeds at five resolutions and four pass settings, then compares shared locations on generated and fixed analytic beds. These checks demonstrate conservative accounting and downstream consistency, **not** satisfactory erosion patterns or resolution convergence. Upstream resolution stability and controlled coefficient/cap sweeps remain open before the heuristic can be treated as a calibrated planet model. Later changes to an already wet world must retain its resolved water volume; they cannot silently refit a coverage target.
